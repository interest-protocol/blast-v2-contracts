# Sui migration venue fee economics

Research snapshot: 2026-09-05. This evaluates the proposed auto-LP distributor and a possible Blast constant-product AMM; it does not select a new migration destination or change executable policy. Published documentation describes defaults, not necessarily a particular pool's current settings. No live Blast deployment configuration or realized volume was established in this research.

## What fees we actually receive

Keep four quantities separate: the trader's pool fee, the external venue's share of that fee, our position's share of the remaining LP earnings, and Blast's creator/protocol division of the amount collected. Aggregator integrator fees are a fifth, independent revenue stream.

For an ordinary CLMM, the approximate accounting is `routed volume × pool fee × (1 − venue cut) × our active-liquidity share`, before Blast's own split. The position's active share can change during a swap and differs from its share of total pool TVL. This is gross fee revenue, not LP profit or return on capital; it excludes changing inventory value, incentives, gas and execution losses.

## Compiled policy examples, not live settings

The checked-in migration policy chooses high trader fees. MMT's `20_000` is 2%, and Cetus's `40_000` is 4%, using their SDKs' verified `1_000_000` denominator. At the documented 20% venue cut, a hypothetical $1,000 of routed input therefore contributes $4 or $8 to the respective venue and $16 or $32 to active LPs collectively. Our vault only earns its active position share, and Blast then applies its own runtime split. [MMT denominator](https://github.com/mmt-finance/clmm-sdk/blob/main/src/utils/constants.ts), [Cetus denominator](https://github.com/CetusProtocol/cetus-clmm-sui-sdk/blob/main/src/types/constants.ts).

The companion mainnet-bytecode audit independently confirmed the same millionths denominator in Bluefin's `clmm_math::fee_rate_denominator` and Turbos's `math_swap::compute_swap`. Their compiled defaults therefore mean 1% for Bluefin (`10_000`) and 4% for Turbos (`40_000`). Bluefin's documented default 20% venue cut would mean $2 to the venue and $8 to active LPs collectively per hypothetical $1,000 routed input. Turbos's net split remains unverified. See [live evidence](sui-auto-lp-venue-evidence.json); defaults and overrides remain owned by the [migrator source](https://github.com/interest-protocol/blast-v2-move/blob/main/sui/migrators/sources/venue_config.move).

Aftermath's checked-in policy uses `100_000_000_000_000 / 10^18 = 0.01%` base swap-in fee and `200 / 10_000 = 2%` DAO fee. The 2% is **an additional wrapper rate, not Aftermath retaining 2% of LP earnings**. The docs' 0.005% base protocol charge is another component. Exact all-in cost depends on fee ordering, charged amounts and rounding; summing percentages is only a rough illustration. These are compiled policy choices, not measured settings for a deployed Blast pool. See [Aftermath policy source](../../sui/migrators/sources/aftermath.move).

The accompanying live-bytecode deposit investigation found a material auto-LP issue: Aftermath's wrapper collects its DAO fee on both offered coin balances **before** the underlying proportional deposit computes utilization. Passing every idle coin on every attempt can therefore charge retained excess repeatedly. Fee-aware sizing must occur before entering this wrapper; skipping after a deposit has already assessed fees is too late. This also overrides any broad reading of the base AMM documentation's statement that deposits are free: the optional wrapper has its own fees.

## Aggregation and an owned constant-product AMM

Aggregators route trades among supported pools; they do not move our LP position across venues or give our creator a claim on other people's positions. Aftermath documents multi-pool routing and per-protocol/pool filters. Cetus documents selectable providers and split routes. Consequently, an owned AMM would require quote/indexing and transaction integration into those providers' supported routes; deployment alone does not establish routing support or volume. This is an inference from their explicit provider model. [Aftermath router](https://docs.aftermath.finance/developers/aftermath-ts-sdk/products/router), [Cetus route model](https://cetus-1.gitbook.io/cetus-developer-docs/developer/cetus-aggregator/features-available).

Integrator fees apply when a trader uses our configured transaction path. Cetus distinguishes a Cetus-pool partner fee from an additional overlay fee across all supported paths. Aftermath says its router has no standalone protocol fee when no integrator fee is configured, but retains 2.5% **of an optional platform fee** when one is set. Neither model grants fees from arbitrary trading of our token elsewhere. Underlying pool fees still apply. [Cetus overlay](https://cetus-1.gitbook.io/cetus-developer-docs/developer/cetus-aggregator/features-available/overlay-fee), [Aftermath router fees](https://docs.aftermath.finance/trade/smart-order-router/fees).

An owned V2-style AMM can make our launch liquidity the default destination and enforce a creator/protocol/LP fee allocation on every swap through that AMM. It cannot force all future liquidity or trading for an ordinary transferable coin to use it. The usual constant-product design retains LP fees in reserves: that portion compounds as swaps execute, without claiming and depositing fees in a second transaction. A separate distributable creator fee would require an explicit accounting design; it does not follow automatically from adopting V2 mathematics. The original Uniswap V2 design is a reference mechanism, not a statement about today's Uniswap fee activation. [V2 whitepaper](https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf).

The decision is whether owning custody, fee policy and the migration destination justifies building and securing the AMM and obtaining routing support. Avoid deciding from nominal venue cuts alone: compare executable quotes at representative trade sizes, active depth, routed volume and net fees under the intended settings. Keeping 100% of a smaller fee stream can earn less than retaining 80% of a larger one.
